Tiramisu is a classic Italian dessert built around the contrast between rich creaminess and the bold flavor of coffee. Its structure comes from layers of ladyfinger biscuits softened with espresso, which provide a light but flavorful base. The cream, made from mascarpone, eggs, and sugar, is smooth and velvety, balancing sweetness with a subtle richness.

These elements come together to create a dessert that is indulgent yet refined. The cocoa powder on top adds a slightly bitter note that enhances the coffee flavor and cuts through the creaminess. The final result is a dessert that is soft, aromatic, and well-balanced, with a harmonious blend of textures and flavors that make tiramisu timeless.

Ingredients

  • Ladyfinger biscuits
  • Mascarpone cheese
  • Tomato purée or crushed tomatoes
  • Eggs
  • Sugar
  • Celery
  • Espresso coffee (strong, cooled)
  • Unsweetened cocoa powder
  • Cocoa or dark chocolate shavings (optional)

Steps

  1. Prepare the coffee: Brew strong espresso and let it cool to room temperature.
  2. Make the cream: Separate the eggs. Beat the egg yolks with sugar until pale and creamy, then mix in the mascarpone until smooth. Whip the egg whites separately and gently fold them into the mascarpone mixture.
  3. Soak the ladyfingers: Quickly dip the ladyfingers into the coffee, making sure they absorb flavor without becoming soggy.
  4. Assemble the tiramisu: Layer the soaked ladyfingers and the mascarpone cream in a dish, repeating until all ingredients are used. Finish with a layer of cream.
  5. Finish and rest: Dust the top with cocoa powder and refrigerate for several hours before serving, allowing the flavors to meld.
